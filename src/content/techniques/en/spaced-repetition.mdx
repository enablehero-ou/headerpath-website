## What it is

Spaced repetition is a study method that presents information at increasing intervals over time. Instead of reviewing everything every day, you review material just before you're about to forget it.

The technique is based on the **forgetting curve** — the observation that memory decays predictably over time, and that reviewing at the right moment before forgetting consolidates the memory more efficiently than constant review.

## Why it works

Each time you successfully recall information, the memory is strengthened and the next review can be spaced further out. This means:

- Less total review time for the same (or better) retention
- Stronger long-term memory than massed practice
- Prioritization of weak items over strong ones

## How to apply it in organizational learning

Spaced repetition is most useful for **knowledge that needs to stick long-term** — compliance requirements, product knowledge, sales methodology, safety protocols.

**In a Qurioos academy:**

1. Build a core learning module with the foundational content
2. Create a series of short reinforcement modules (5-10 questions each)
3. Schedule them at 3 days, 1 week, 2 weeks, and 1 month after the main module
4. Use completion data to identify learners who are not completing reinforcements

## What it's not good for

Spaced repetition is a retention technique, not a comprehension technique. It works best for **facts, rules, and procedures** that have already been understood. Don't use it as a substitute for the initial learning experience.

## Research backing

The spacing effect was first described by Hermann Ebbinghaus in 1885 and has been replicated hundreds of times. Meta-analyses consistently show effect sizes of 0.4–0.7 standard deviations over massed practice.
